How the instrument thinks

It doesn't ask what she already mastered.
It asks what she's just starting to learn.

Questions about things every baby already does teach us nothing. So Kinedu asks about what's just appearing — skills about half of children that age have. That means many honest answers are “not yet”. And that's not behind — that's exactly where the interesting things are happening.

What we found

Four findings, each with its number and its limit.

From our 2026 study, Coupling and Differentiation in Early Development. Four things the data showed — each with its number, and with what it does not mean.

Expected ages match reality. Every milestone's expected age was checked against ~280 million in-scope observations.

median error: 0 months vs the empirical 75th percentile

In year one, development moves as one connected system. First described by Stanford researchers in Kinedu data; reproduced by Kinedu across ~1.7 million children.

within-child coupling across areas, replicated at 2-, 4- and 6-month intervals

Around age one, the areas begin keeping their own time. Coupling softens, but it does not vanish.

connection roughly halves at the transition: 0.49 → 0.27

A starting point is not a destiny. Where a child starts helps explain where they stand later, but says almost nothing about how fast they move next.

37% of later standing · 1.5% of subsequent pace

None of this shows that a parent action or any product caused development to change, and we won't pretend otherwise.

What the record shows

Some skills hand off. Others stay for life.

A skill's window isn't just when it appears. It's when it's actively being built. Some skills are stepping stones that get replaced by something more advanced. Others, once gained, stay. The assessment knows the difference, and stops asking about what a child has already moved past.

Babbling hands off to first words.

Babbling builds early: half of children by 5 months. As children move toward first words, the assessment shifts its attention from babbling to early speech.

Share of children showing each skill, by age · Kinedu record, 2020–2023 · shaded band ±1 SD across the skill's milestones

Walking arrives, and stays.

Half of children by 19 months, three quarters by 29, and once gained, never handed off. The wide range is real: children reach it at genuinely different ages.

Our honesty rules

A useful guide. Not a diagnosis.

Kinedu is a developmental check-in for everyday observation and practical support. It gives context; it does not replace a conversation with a qualified professional.

Three · Normal is wide

The gate: the span between the first quarter of children to get a skill and the last quarter. Later skills simply have a far wider normal.
