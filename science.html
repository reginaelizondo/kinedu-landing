How the instrument thinks

It doesn't ask what your child already does.
It asks right at the edge.

A question every child passes teaches us nothing about yours. The informative questions are the ones about half of children pass at that age, which means that for a perfectly typical child many honest answers are “not yet”. That's the instrument working, not your child falling short.

Why 32 questions

Reliability 0.92, right where the curve flattens.

Eight questions per area reach 0.87. Thirty-two reach 0.92. Answering 48 barely adds anything, so we stop where your time stops paying for precision. The free reading is the most reliable number the product produces.

What the record shows

Some skills hand off. Others stay for life.

A skill's window isn't just when it appears. It's when it's actively being built. Some skills are stepping stones that get replaced by something more advanced. Others, once gained, stay. The assessment knows the difference, and stops asking about what a child has already moved past.

Babbling hands off to first words.

Babbling builds early: half of children by 5 months. As children move toward first words, the assessment shifts its attention from babbling to early speech.

Share of children showing each skill, by age · Kinedu record, 2020–2023 · shaded band ±1 SD across the skill's milestones

Walking arrives, and stays.

Half of children by 19 months, three quarters by 29, and once gained, never handed off. The wide range is real: children reach it at genuinely different ages.

Our honesty rules

What this science refuses to do.

Every rule below exists because of a real mistake we made and fixed. That's what it means to be built on evidence.

✓

It never diagnoses. It describes where a child is today and what usually comes next: developmental surveillance, not a medical label.

✓

It celebrates upward, never alarms downward. A mistaken compliment doesn't hurt a family. A mistaken alarm does.

✓

It shows no number it can't sustain. When eight answers aren't enough to place an area, it says “more answers needed”, never a scary zero.

✓

Born early? It says so. Ages are corrected for prematurity using the American Academy of Pediatrics equation, and the reading declares it, every time.

✓

It compares against a fixed norm. Your child is never ranked against other users, only placed on a frozen population reference, like a pediatrician's growth chart.

✓

It won't promise what it can't measure. It doesn't score your parenting, and it doesn't pretend one snapshot defines a child. Development keeps unfolding.
