Big Bang Baby

The Science of How Your Child’s Universe Takes Shape

Luis Garza Sada, founder of Kinedu

Every child is a universe being born.

What sixteen years of Kinedu, four daughters and the record of 3.5 million children taught Luis about the first years.

The big idea

Your baby grows like the universe.

Mind, body, language and emotions are not four separate tracks. Early on they move together. Then they expand, cool, and take shape.

In the beginning, everything is everything.

In the first months every skill touches every other one. A game of peekaboo is building language, memory and trust at the same time.

It expands.

Connections multiply faster than at any other time in life. Windows open. Earlier is better; later is not impossible.

It takes shape.

Around the first birthday, distinct areas emerge and your child starts becoming exactly who they are. Different from every other child, including their sibling.

Seen in the record of 3.5 million children who grew up with Kinedu.

Why this book

Parenting advice got loud. This is the calm.

Contradictory experts, vague slogans, and the fear that one wrong move lasts forever. Luis lived it too, four daughters deep.

From the introduction

It looks like a father sitting on the kitchen floor at 7:15 in the morning, trying to make the school carpool on time while running late for work, “holding space” for a three-year-old who is screaming because her banana broke in half. The father knows, somewhere deep in the rational part of his brain, that this is developmentally normal. He has read the posts. He understands that a three-year-old does not yet have the capacity to regulate an intense emotional response. He is trying to do the right thing.

Meanwhile, three other children need breakfast, school starts in twenty minutes, and the broken banana is now on the floor.

1

You are the adult in the room.

Warmth and structure together, not one or the other. Setting a limit does not break the bond.

2

All feelings are real. Not every reaction is valid.

Helping your child feel an emotion is not the same as accepting whatever that emotion makes them do.

3

A bad day is not trauma.

Normal friction builds resilience. What makes stress safe is a caring adult nearby. That adult is you.
