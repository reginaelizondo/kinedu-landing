The first 1,000 days

Your baby's brain is building itself right now — faster than it ever will again.

At first, everything grows at once — physical, cognitive, language and social-emotional skills move as one. Then, like a universe after the Big Bang, they expand into distinct but deeply connected abilities around 12 months.

And the pace is staggering: about 50 milestones by 1 month, ~300 by age 2. We measured it with Stanford — early on, progress in one ability pulls the others along.

Act 2 · It changes what you do

The evidence no milestone chart can give you.

Measuring development well is one thing. Changing how a parent and child interact is another — and it's the part that's uniquely ours.

Serve and return: how brains are built

Your baby reaches out, you respond — a look, a word, a hug. Scientists call it serve and return: the exchange that builds the brain.

Kinedu is built around it. In a study with Stanford, one short Kinedu activity video led parents to make 69% more bids for joint attention and speak 35% more.

What actually works

The sweet spot: guided play

Children learn most when an adult sets the stage and the child leads the play. Not rigid lessons. Not a free-for-all. The space in between.

Too little structure

Free play

All freedom, no direction. Joyful and essential — but the learning goal can get lost.

Just right

Guided play

An adult sets a goal and offers gentle prompts; the child explores and leads.

Too much structure

Direct instruction

All direction, little agency. Efficient for facts — but it narrows curiosity.

What it will never tell you

A verdict.

  • A diagnosis of a developmental condition or delay
  • A prediction of IQ, school readiness, or "potential"
  • A verdict about your child from a population pattern
  • Anything that replaces your pediatrician
Our point of view

Not perfection. Presence.

The science is reassuring: warm, responsive, "good enough" parenting produces secure, thriving children in the large majority of cases. You don't need to get every moment right — you need to be a present, intentional guide.
